# 004 — Conductors, Insulators, and Semiconductors

Electricity depends not only on electrons, but on how easily those electrons can move through a material.

## Conductors

Conductors allow charge to move relatively easily. Metals such as copper are useful because some electrons are loosely bound and can move through the material when voltage creates an electric field.

## Insulators

Insulators strongly resist the movement of charge. Rubber, glass, and many plastics are used to keep current on its intended path and protect people and components.

## Semiconductors

A semiconductor sits between those extremes. It does not conduct as freely as copper, but its behavior can be deliberately controlled.

Silicon is especially useful because engineers can change how well it conducts by:

- adding carefully selected impurities;
- applying voltage;
- combining differently treated regions;
- controlling temperature and light.

That controllability is the reason semiconductors can become switches.

## Connection to the Next Layer

A pure semiconductor is not yet a useful transistor. First, its electrical behavior must be modified through a process called **doping**.
